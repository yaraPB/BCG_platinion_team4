Humanitarian food logistics · Kaveri region (demo network)

Food reaches people even when the roads don't.

We move relief food from ports, depots and hubs to camps and villages on unstable road networks. When a road is cut we reroute. If the cut leaves a community with no road to its supplier, we find the nearest supplier it can still reach that has stock. Live hazard alerts let us plan for this before the road closes.

How it works

A small set of graph algorithms run continuously. The same engine applies to any logistics network; this demo is set up for food security.

01

Network as a graph

Ports, depots and hubs are supplier nodes; camps and villages are demand nodes; roads are edges weighted by travel time (distance ÷ speed for paved, gravel or track).

02

Detect the split

When a road closes, we recompute connected components. If a destination and its planned supplier are now in different subnetworks, rerouting cannot help, and we search for a new supplier inside the destination's own subnetwork.

03

Reroute or switch supplier

Dijkstra from each destination ranks every reachable supplier. We keep the planned supplier unless it is cut off, out of stock, or its detour is much slower than the nearest alternative. The highest-need communities (IPC phase, days of food left) are assigned stock first.

04

Predict and pre-plan

Weather, river, landslide and security feeds give each road a failure probability. A Monte Carlo simulation of the network produces the playbook and tells us where to pre-position stock before the road closes.
